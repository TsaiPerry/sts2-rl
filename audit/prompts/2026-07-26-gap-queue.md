## What each queue entry needs

- **Stable id**: `<seam>/<step-or-guard-id>`, e.g. `hook_dispatch/G9`.
- **Live or dormant**, taken from the record. For dormant entries, the
  concrete unported thing that would make it live.
- **The divergence in one sentence**, with the sim `file:line` and the C#
  `file:line`.
- **The observable effect** — what a player or a replay would see. Where the
  record has executed numbers, carry them.
- **Its pin**: the `strict=True` xfail in `test/test_hook_order.py` that will
  flip to passing when the gap is fixed, or "unpinned" with the reason.
- **A fix recipe**: which file changes, roughly how, and what the failing
  test would assert. Recipes, not patches — this stream writes no engine code.
- **Blast radius**: other entries that share the mechanism, and any content
  unit the record names as affected.

## Ordering

Sort by **seed-convergence impact first**, since that is the work this
pipeline exists to unblock, then by breadth of blast radius, then by fix
cost. Live gaps above dormant ones throughout.

Some findings you will encounter that illustrate the top of the queue: the
multiplicative-aggregation shape (`hook_dispatch/G9` — the sim applies a
product once where C# threads a running decimal, giving 20 vs 21 on a
Shrink+Vulnerable attack); the missing `CheckWinCondition` after turn-1 setup;
deck transforms bypassing the egg-relic hooks; and the cross-listener
dispatch order. Verify each against the records rather than taking this list
as authoritative — it is a calibration hint, not the answer.

## Group by mechanism, not just by seam

Several gaps are one mechanism recorded at several sites, correctly carrying
the same verdict at each (rule 3). Fixing one may clear three. Make those
groups explicit — a queue that lists them as independent items overstates the
work by a wide margin.
